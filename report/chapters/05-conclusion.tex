This chapter answers the project's question in a few sentences and lists the work that
should come next.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The project asked whether the brain signals of stroke patients, read by an EEG cap, can
tell a robotic sixth finger when to move, and how much practice a new patient must give
before they can. Working on a public recording of \nSubjects{} patients, we reached four
conclusions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The published accuracy was inflated.} The published method let its test
  trials shape its first step. Our rebuild of the authors' recipe scored
  \pct{\rqOneCspLdaReplicaPaperReplicaMean} that way, above the published 85 to 86\,\%, and
  \pct{\rqOneCspLdaReplicaCvLeakfreeMean} without the leak. Evaluated without leakage, a
  standard method told hand and sixth-finger imagery apart
  \pct{\rqOneCspLdaCvLeakfreeMean} of the time, against \pct{\rqOneCspLdaPaperReplicaMean}
  run the published way, and the leak held against run-wise splits too. The same mistake
  raised accuracy on two datasets of healthy people.
  \item \textbf{One transfer configuration gave a quick start, then stayed flat.} A model
  re-centred on the other patients, with each of the new patient's windows counting as one
  of theirs, reached \pct{\rqTwoRestAhSfRctTslrKOneMean} on three choices after one
  practice trial of each movement, about \rqTwoMinutesKOne{} minutes, where the same model
  trained from scratch needed about \rqTwoMinutesKTen{} minutes (\rqTwoMinutesRecordedKTen{}
  as recorded) to get there. It then stayed nearly flat, and with the whole practice pool
  the patient's own model ended numerically higher, a difference we did not test. Both
  pre-registered findings, the quick start and its shrinking with the whole pool, were
  replicated after therapy. They describe this weighting, not transfer in general.
  \item \textbf{Offline, the sixth finger is a candidate switch.} Sixth-finger imagery
  against rest was recognised \pct{\exFourTsLrCentralMean} of the time window by window,
  but the same switch also responded to imagined hand movements, and played continuously
  it caught only \pct{\exFiveFingerFingerOnMean} of the sixth-finger periods, with
  \exFiveFingerFalsePerMinMean{} false switch-ons per minute of rest. It was never tested
  with a patient in the loop. The data show a candidate ``move now'' command, not a
  command distinct from the hand. A model also needs a short top-up of practice on each
  new day.
  \item \textbf{The pipeline works end to end.} The trained model runs in any browser on a
  real patient's held-out recording and moves a drawn hand with a sixth finger, at
  \url{\siteURL}.
\end{enumerate}

The project's lasting contribution is a leakage-free benchmark of this dataset and a
pre-registered measurement of calibration for a new kind of command. The data are public,
and the code, the frozen plan and its deviation log are available from the authors on
request, so that both can be checked.

\section{Future Scope}\label{sec:future}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A closed-loop study.} Patients would see or feel the sixth finger respond
  while they practise. Feedback lets people adapt to the decoder, and it would test the
  switch where it matters.
  \item \textbf{A better switch.} Fewer false movements at rest are the first need. Options
  include a threshold tuned on each patient's practice runs and a decoder trained for rest
  against any movement.
  \item \textbf{Transfer that adapts as practice grows.} Giving the new patient's own trials
  more weight as they accumulate could keep the quick start without the plateau.
  \item \textbf{Other patients and centres.} Other public stroke datasets would show whether
  the results hold beyond one hospital~\cite{liu2024acute,lu2026chronic,liu2025lowerlimb}.
  \item \textbf{Large pre-trained EEG models.} Models trained on very large collections of EEG
  may transfer better than models trained on the patients of one study~\cite{jiang2024labram,wang2025cbramod}.
  \item \textbf{A physical finger.} The command already leaves the decoder in a standard
  form; a microcontroller with joint limits and an emergency stop, live EEG and causal
  filters would connect it to a real robotic finger (Section~\ref{sec:limb}).
  \item \textbf{Fewer electrodes.} The switch was read at least as well from the 14
  central electrodes as from all 40 (\pct{\rqFourCueSfRestCentralMean} against
  \pct{\rqFourCueSfRestAllChannelsMean}), which points toward a smaller cap that a patient
  could wear at home.
\end{enumerate}
