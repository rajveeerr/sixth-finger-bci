\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Evaluated without leakage, one re-centred transfer configuration gave stroke
patients a fast start on a sixth-finger BCI, lifting three-class accuracy
from \rqTwoRestAhSfTsLrKOneMean\,\% to \rqTwoRestAhSfRctTslrKOneMean\,\% after
one trial of each movement, and then stayed nearly flat; with the whole pool
(\rqTwoMinutesKAll\,min nominal, \rqTwoMinutesRecordedKAll\,min as recorded)
a decoder trained on the patient alone ended numerically, not significantly,
higher. No distinct sixth-finger command was shown. The only contrast these
data support offline is imagery against rest, a candidate ``move now''
switch; replayed continuously it caught \exFiveFingerFingerOnMean\,\% of
sixth-finger periods at \exFiveFingerFalsePerMinMean{} false activations per
minute, and it needs closed-loop testing.
