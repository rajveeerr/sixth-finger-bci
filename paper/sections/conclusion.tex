\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Tested fairly, a sixth-finger BCI for stroke patients gains a fast start from
other patients' data: one trial of each movement lifted three-class
accuracy from \rqTwoRestAhSfTsLrKOneMean\,\% to
\rqTwoRestAhSfRctTslrKOneMean\,\%. Transfer then levels off, and a decoder trained on the
patient alone overtakes it once the whole pool, \rqTwoMinutesKAll\,min of trials, is
recorded. Transfer gave no clear benefit for adding the sixth finger to a
hand decoder, and the sixth finger was not shown to differ from imagined hand
movement. It works best as a single ``move now'' switch, recalibrated each
day.
