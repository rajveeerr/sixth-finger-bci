\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{A fast start from one transfer configuration.} Re-centred transfer with
a target weight of \rqTwoHparamRctTslrWeight{} gave a new patient, after one
trial of each movement (\rqTwoMinutesKOne\,min), the three-class accuracy that
the same classifier from scratch reached after ten trials of each movement
(\rqTwoMinutesKTen\,min nominal, \rqTwoMinutesRecordedKTen\,min as recorded),
and then stayed nearly flat; with the whole pool the decoder from scratch
ended numerically higher, a difference we did not test. Transfer shortens
calibration for healthy users~\cite{zanini2018,he2020,wu2022review} and, across
sessions, for stroke patients~\cite{giles2022,cao2021}; here, from other
patients, it helped for the first few trials. These curves describe this
configuration, not transfer in general. Each of the patient's windows weighed
as much as one of the other patients' thousands, a weight chosen from
candidates that all scored near chance, and the deep pair was likewise set to
its least adaptive values. A weight that grows with $k$, a switch to the patient's own decoder chosen by cross-validation
within the calibration data, or Procrustes alignment~\cite{rodrigues2019} may
let transfer keep learning; we did not test them. Part of the early lead
reflects how little a from-scratch decoder learns from a few windows.

\emph{Leakage.} The inflation is a property of the procedure: the same code
inflated accuracy on two healthy datasets. It says nothing about the quality
of the recordings, and the release of the data and code is what made the
check possible. We did not reproduce the published value, and the inflation
held against all three leakage-free estimates. Users of the dataset should
fit every supervised step inside the training folds, split by run, and report
the leakage-free figure next to the published one. Evaluated without leakage,
hand and sixth finger are only weakly separable in this EEG, and part of that
separation may be visual.

\emph{Imagery against rest, not yet a sixth finger.} No distinct sixth-finger
command was shown, and none was ruled out. The pre-registered control found
that a decoder which never saw a sixth-finger trial detects one as well, and
the detectors fire for imagined hand movement too. Mu desynchronisation at C3
was slightly stronger for the sixth finger (\edaErdMuCThreeSfMean\,\% against
\edaErdMuCThreeAhMean\,\%, $p = \edaErdMuCThreeSfMinusAhP$,
$n = \edaErdMuCThreeN$), an observation made before the pre-registration,
which declared that it would not be tested again; a difference in strength
alone would not make the two separable. The only contrast these data support
offline is imagery against rest, a candidate ``move now'' switch. Played
continuously it caught fewer than half of the sixth-finger periods, late in
the period, and also switched on for hand imagery, so whether a patient could use
it is a question for a closed-loop study. It needs new calibration each day;
the patient's earlier session topped up with five trials of each movement,
pooled or re-centred, is the practical form of the fast start.

\emph{Limitations.} The \nSubjects{} patients came from one centre, which
limits how far these results generalise, and between sessions they received
one of two therapies. All evaluation is offline or pseudo-online (replayed),
never closed-loop with a patient. The novel command was imagined, with the
robotic finger worn but not driven by the decoder. We show per-patient
results (Figs.~\ref{fig:leak}, \ref{fig:rqthree}, \ref{fig:later}
and~\ref{fig:switch}, the counts of $k^*$), not only means. The from-scratch
baselines are weak at small budgets by design, with four windows at $k = 1$
and, for a new command, unbalanced classes without class weights; a
regularised or class-weighted baseline may narrow transfer's early lead. Two
limitations were declared in the pre-registration. Rest windows may
contain the beta rebound that follows imagery, so accuracies that involve
rest are upper bounds for self-paced rest. The difference in
desynchronisation between sixth finger and hand is spatially diffuse, so
sixth-finger decodability may reflect the intensity of imagery rather than a
distinct motor map. Rest was also always shown as a cross and imagery as a
picture: the switch draws mostly on central channels, but a visual part is
not ruled out, and the hand-against-finger signal is at least as strong over
the visual cortex. The authors' preprocessing included manual steps that
cannot be repeated exactly. With \nPre{} patients the tests can detect only
medium or large effects, so the inconclusive H3 and H3c are not evidence of
no effect.

\emph{Future work.} A closed-loop study in which the decoder drives the
finger is the next test. Other stroke datasets~\cite{liu2024acute,lu2026chronic}
and EEG foundation models~\cite{jiang2024labram,wang2025cbramod} may lift the
plateau; pre-training on healthy people's imagery did not, in a first
exploratory attempt.
