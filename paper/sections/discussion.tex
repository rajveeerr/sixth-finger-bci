\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Transfer gives a fast start, not a better decoder.} With transfer,
\rqTwoMinutesKOne\,min of trials gave a new patient the three-class accuracy
that a decoder from scratch reached only after ten trials of each movement,
\rqTwoMinutesKTen\,min. By ten trials the lead was gone, and with the whole pool the
decoder from scratch was ahead. Transfer shortens calibration for healthy
users of conventional commands~\cite{zanini2018,he2020,wu2022review}; here it
did so only for the first few trials. These curves suggest starting a session
with transfer and switching to the patient's own decoder once about ten
trials of each movement are recorded. We did not test that schedule.

\emph{Why transfer levels off.} Our reading, which we did not test, is that
the other patients' thousands of windows outweigh the patient's few dozen,
so the classifier stays mostly theirs. The pre-registered rule chose the
smallest target weight, on two patients whose candidates scored within a
point of each other; a weight that grows with $k$ may let transfer keep
learning. Stroke also makes patients unlike each other, since EEG responses
vary with lesion location~\cite{park2016}, and mirroring lesions to one side
did not help. The deep pair levelled off the same way, so the plateau is not
specific to one classifier.

\emph{The fair test.} The inflation is a property of the procedure: the same
code inflated accuracy on two healthy datasets. It says nothing about the
quality of the recordings, and the release of the data and code is what made
the check possible. Users of the dataset should fit every supervised step
inside the training folds, split by run, and report the fair figure next to
the published one. Tested fairly, hand and sixth finger are only weakly
separable in this EEG.

\emph{A switch, not yet a sixth finger.} No distinct sixth-finger command was
shown, and none was ruled out. The pre-registered control found that a
decoder which never saw a sixth-finger trial detects one as well, and the
detectors fire for imagined hand movement too. Mu desynchronisation at C3 was
slightly stronger for the sixth finger (\edaErdMuCThreeSfMean\,\% against
\edaErdMuCThreeAhMean\,\%, $p = \edaErdMuCThreeSfMinusAhP$,
$n = \edaErdMuCThreeN$, exploratory), but
a difference in strength alone would not make the two separable. What the
data support is one ``move now'' switch. It also needs new calibration each
day; the patient's own earlier session, re-centred and topped up with five
trials of each movement, is the practical form of the fast start.

\emph{Limitations.} The \nSubjects{} patients came from one centre, which
limits how far these results generalise. All evaluation is offline or
pseudo-online (replayed), never closed-loop with a patient. The novel
command was imagined, with the robotic finger worn but not driven by the
decoder. We show per-patient results (Figs.~\ref{fig:leak},
\ref{fig:rqthree} and~\ref{fig:switch}, the counts of $k^*$), not only means. Two limitations
were declared in the pre-registration. Rest windows may contain the beta
rebound that follows imagery, so accuracies that involve rest are upper
bounds for self-paced rest. The difference in desynchronisation between
sixth finger and hand is spatially diffuse, so sixth-finger decodability may
reflect the intensity of imagery rather than a distinct motor map. Rest was
also recorded with a cross on screen and imagery with a picture. The switch
was carried more by the central channels (\rqFourCueSfRestCentralMean\,\%)
than by five parieto-occipital ones (\rqFourCueSfRestParietoOccipitalMean\,\%),
so the picture is not its main source, but a visual part is not ruled out.
The authors' preprocessing included manual steps that cannot be repeated
exactly. With \nPre{} patients the tests can detect only medium or large
effects, so the inconclusive H3 and H3c are not evidence of no effect.

\emph{Future work.} A closed-loop study in which the decoder drives the
finger is the next test. Other stroke datasets~\cite{liu2024acute,lu2026chronic},
EEG foundation models~\cite{jiang2024labram,wang2025cbramod} and pre-training
on healthy people's imagery (a first exploratory attempt did not help) may
lift the plateau.
